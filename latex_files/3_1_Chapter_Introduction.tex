\chapter{Introduction}
The pursuit of understanding the fundamental building blocks of the Universe has long been a central goal of physics. Over the past century, significant progress has been made in describing the elementary particles, their interactions, and the underlying fields.
Despite these achievements, several important open questions remain, including the nature of dark matter and the hierarchy problem associated with the Higgs boson. These open questions provide strong motivation for the continuing investigation into the fundamental structure of the Universe.

The ATLAS detector is designed to study these building blocks in an effort to address such questions. As a general purpose detector at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), ATLAS records proton-proton collisions at center-of-mass energies of up to 13.6 \TeV. The data collected from ATLAS is used to perform precision measurements of Standard Model and to search for evidence of physics beyond the Standard Model.


This dissertation presents work on both physics analyses and detector upgrade tasks. 
The two analyses presented in this dissertation search for signatures of disappearing tracks and the combination of the disappearing tracks with ionization energy loss. 
Both analyses target the direct detection of charged long-lived particles predicted in supersymmetric models. 
The detector upgrade work focuses on the ATLAS Inner Tracker, contributing to preparations for the High-Luminosity LHC era and ensuring the continued performance of the ATLAS detector with higher pileup and integrated luminosity.
