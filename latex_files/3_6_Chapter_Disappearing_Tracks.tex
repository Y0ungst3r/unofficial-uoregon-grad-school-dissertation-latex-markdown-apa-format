\section{Preface}
The proceeding sections of this chapter are from published works by the ATLAS collaboration \cite{atlascollaboration2026searchlonglivedcharginostausleptons}. The work contributing to this dissertation as part of this search focuses primarily on the addition of two slepton models. In particular, the \stau GMSB and \stau co annihilation models as described in Sec.~\ref{sec:SUSY}, their addition to the disappearing track search, slepton generation in a model never explored on ATLAS, the validation of the samples, checking of event selection in comparison to the \chinopm models, and event reweighting. 

This work included the generation of \stau CMSSM coannihilation samples based on the model described in Sec. \ref{sec:CMSSM_stau}. These samples had not previously been produced within ATLAS.

Due to the requirement that disappearing tracks decay inside the ID, the shortest lifetime samples were obtained by reweighting \stau signal samples from a lifetime of 1 ns down to 0.02 ns to allow them to cover the same region as the \chinopm models.

Both \stau models (GMSB and CMSSM coannihilation) were compared to the \chinopm models to verify that the selection efficiency was consistent across all signal models.

Finally, the validation regions were checked for signal contamination. It was found that the \stau model exhibits significant contamination in the MET 150–300 GeV validation region. As a result, this region was changed to a signal region SR prior to unblinding.

Lastly the \stau GMSB model has been included in the summary plot produced by ATLAS, Fig. \ref{fig:SummaryStau}
